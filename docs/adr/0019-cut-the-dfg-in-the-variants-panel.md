# The DFG is cut in the Variants panel, before the build

The Behaviour and Paths sliders used to cut the drawing only. `simplify()` hid Variants, activities and edges from the graph Rust had built, but every Significance Test, and the Benjamini-Hochberg correction across each attribute's family, had been computed over every Variant the panel selected. The tests panel listed only the places on screen, yet each test described cases that were no longer drawn, and whether a place read as significant depended on places the reader could not see. The tests could not be trusted to mean what the picture suggested.

**Both sliders now stage Variants in the Variants panel.** Behaviour is the coverage slider the panel already had for the tree: the most common Variants up to a share of the cases. Paths keeps, out of those, only the Variants every one of whose transitions survives the busiest-path cut: each Group's busiest share of transitions plus every activity's busiest way in and way out, the same cut `simplify()` used to draw. A Variant that runs a rarer transition is left out whole. Apply sends the staged keys to `dfg`, which already cuts before aggregating, and the canvas draws every Variant the graph was built from. What is drawn, what is counted and what is tested are the same cases.

Paths could have trimmed the cut transitions out of each trace instead. That was rejected because a trimmed trace runs pairs no case ran: dropping B from A, B, C draws A to C. It could also have stayed a drawing-only cut, labelled as such, but then the tests panel would still describe edges and cases off the screen.

**Moving a slider stages; it does not redraw.** The panel reports the staged Variants, their share of cases, and the activities and paths they would draw; the graph changes on Apply, like every other edit the panel holds. A preview would show a graph whose tests had not been recomputed, which is the disagreement this change removes.

**The selection seeds from the coverage default.** An empty `dfg_settings` selection now means "not chosen yet", as it does for the tree, and the first load stages the Variants holding 80% of the cases. Drawing every Variant by default would have made the first graph of a large log the slowest and least readable one. `load()` reads the Variant list before every build, so a selection is seeded and pruned before Rust sees it. A project that had stored an empty selection, meaning every Variant, is seeded the same way on its next load.

**Consequence:** the sliders' positions are not persisted. The selection they staged is, in `dfg_settings`, so a reload draws the same graph, but Paths reads 100% again and Behaviour reads the coverage the selection holds. Picking a row by hand after moving a slider keeps the slider where it is, and moving a slider again restages from the two and drops the hand-picked rows. Both split panels are still cut by the one pair, now through the one selection.
