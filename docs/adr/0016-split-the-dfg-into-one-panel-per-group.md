# The DFG splits into one panel per Group, both drawn from one cut

A comparative DFG prints both Groups' figures on every node face, which answers "how do the two differ here" but never "what shape does each Group actually have". Reading one Group's flow meant counting past the other's numbers on every node.

The DFG can now be split: the same graph twice, one Group to a panel, toggled from the **Split by group** field in the toolbar. The field is disabled unless exactly two Groups are compared, and turning it on removes no Group, so a comparison that falls back to one Group shows the single graph again and comes back split when the second returns.

**Both panels are one drawing, not two.** The toolbar holds one Behaviour slider and one Paths slider, and both panels are cut by them. The layout is computed once, from the whole cut rather than from what either panel draws, so an activity sits at the same coordinates on both sides; the two panels then share one viewport, so panning or zooming either moves both. A panel drops the nodes and edges its own Group never reaches, which is what makes the two shapes comparable at a glance, and the boxes it drops stay in the placement, so nothing else moves.

An earlier attempt gave each panel its own pair of sliders with a pair of **Sync** toggles over the divider. Two panels that could be cut differently cannot share a layout, so the toggles also decided whether the coordinates lined up, and the first thing a reader had to work out was which of four states they were in. One cut for both is the whole feature: the panels differ in what each Group does, not in how each was cut.

**Both panels are grey.** An activity reads in the Original's grey on both sides, and only an activity that panel's Group alone reaches carries that Group's accent. The same attempt had painted every node in its panel's Group colour and marked the exclusive ones with a star in the corner: two encodings of membership, neither readable, and the thing worth seeing drowned in the thing that was true everywhere. Grey-unless-exclusive leaves the accent meaning one thing.

**The sliders live in the toolbar.** They used to float over the canvas, where they covered the graph and each panel needed its own. The figures they printed under them — variants, activities and cases kept — are now a tooltip on an info icon beside each slider, so the row stays the height of the toolbar.

**Fitting the view.** A cut changes how much graph there is to frame, so moving either slider refits. Two things make that awkward: ELK is asynchronous, so the placement a fit is meant to frame lands a tick after the topology changes, and Svelte Flow's own Controls are drawn inside the flow's box, so a fit that only knows the box centres the graph underneath them. The canvas counts placements and holds a requested fit until the placement it was asked for has landed, then fits inside `fitViewOptions` padding that insets the left by the Controls' width. `refit.svelte` exists because `useSvelteFlow` only resolves inside `SvelteFlow`, and it waits one `tick` so the nodes its sibling effect seeds are in the DOM before anything is measured. In a split, only the panel that fits runs this; the other follows through the shared viewport.

**Consequence:** `DfgView` carries one `coverage` and one `paths` for every panel, so nothing in the view can express two different cuts, which is what keeps the panels comparable. A reader who wants to cut one Group alone has to compare it against the Original instead. The split is view state, not persisted: it is lost on reload, like the sliders and the direction.
